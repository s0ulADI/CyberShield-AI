Core idea

Cybercrime succeeds when trust is converted into action.

The attacker may use a fake website, a compromised email, a cloned support call, or a stolen signing key. The victim is pushed to click, pay, approve, install, or reveal. This project studies those moments through short cases and investigation clues.

Attack chain

Most cases follow the same human-to-digital path.

01

Target

A person, student, customer, employee, or business role is selected.

02

Contact

Email, SMS, call, direct message, fake app, or cloned website appears.

03

Manipulate

Fear, urgency, authority, greed, romance, or curiosity is applied.

04

Exploit

Credentials, OTP, payment approval, document signature, or access is captured.

05

Hide

Funds move through mule accounts, logs are deleted, or identities are masked.

Mini cases

Illustrations from common cybercrime scenarios.

Social Engineering

Fake IT Helpdesk Call

A caller pretends to be from IT and pressures a user to approve an MFA login.

Red flags:

Urgency, authority pressure, request for OTP or MFA approval, refusal to use official support channels.

Control:

Verify through official helpdesk numbers and use phishing-resistant MFA where possible.

Financial Fraud

Business Email Compromise

An accountant receives a fake vendor email asking to update bank details.

Red flags:

Changed account number, urgent payment deadline, slightly altered email domain.

Control:

Call the vendor using a known number and require two-person payment approval.

Online Scam

Fake Delivery Fee Link

A message says a parcel is stuck and asks for a small payment to release it.

Red flags:

Unknown short link, low fee bait, card details requested, generic greeting.

Control:

Track parcels only through the official courier app or website.

Digital Signature

Misused Signing Token

A digital signature certificate is used to approve a fake document without consent.

Investigation clues:

Token access, signing timestamp, device logs, user login, CCTV, document audit trail.

Control:

Never share token passwords; revoke compromised certificates quickly.

Certificate Crime

Certificate Authority Compromise

A trusted issuer is breached and fraudulent certificates can impersonate websites.

Impact:

Users may believe a fake or intercepted website is secure because the certificate appears trusted.

Control:

Strong CA security, auditing, certificate transparency, and rapid revocation.

Digital Forensics

Metadata Reveals Authorship

An anonymous document contains hidden metadata linking it to a device or user.

Evidence:

File metadata, timestamps, author fields, email headers, deleted fragments.

Control:

Preserve evidence with proper collection and chain of custody.

Financial fraud map

How cyber fraud turns a message into money loss.

1

Bait

Fake bank alert, delivery issue, investment promise, job offer, or support call.

2

Trust hook

Known brand name, authority, emergency, discount, or emotional pressure.

3

Capture

Victim shares OTP, logs into fake page, installs remote app, or sends payment.

4

Move funds

Money is moved through accounts, wallets, vouchers, or crypto channels.

Digital forensics

Investigators rebuild the story from digital traces.

Digital forensics protects evidence, extracts useful information, and explains what happened in a way that can support incident response or legal action.

Identify

Find relevant devices, accounts, files, logs, messages, and transactions.

Acquire

Collect evidence carefully without changing it.

Analyze

Build timelines, recover artifacts, compare logs, and connect events.

Report

Document findings, methods, limitations, and evidence chain.
